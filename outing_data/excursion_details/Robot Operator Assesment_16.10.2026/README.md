This is my first overnight excursion, and I’m writing this in real time as it happens. So expect random updates, questionable observations, travel frustrations, and probably some overthinking. Let’s see how this goes.


## October 15 — The journey begins

**2:30 p.m. — Finally ready to leave**

I had set my alarm for 7 a.m. I woke up at 9am.

It then took me five and a half hours to shower, eat breakfast and lunch, pack, and prepare food for the trip. I think I finally understand why my mother worries about me.

For food, I packed four potato sandwiches, six peeled carrots, three protein bars, a packet of Monaco biscuits, salted peanuts, and a packet of Lay’s. Hopefully, that will last me the entire outing. For cloths I  packed a pyjamas for the night, a shirt and pant for interview and a loose tshirt as i was asked to bring. I am wearing my white sneakers which I plan to wear to the assessment,black loose trouser which i might need tomorrow for the assesment,a random tshirt and my everyday jacket . And, of course, my portable bidet.


**6:00 p.m. — Somewhere on the RE8**

I'm on the RE8 towards Stuttgart and have just reached Lauda station. The journey is officially underway!

**8:15 p.m. — Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof**

I'm waiting for the U-Bahn. I need to take the U12 to reach my hotel, but to get to the platform, I have to leave the main station first.

The moment I stepped outside and saw Stuttgart, I had this strange feeling. You know those stories where a boy from a village arrives in a big city for the first time and everything feels enormous and unfamiliar?

That was me.


**9:00 p.m. — Hostel check-in complete**

I've freshened up and settled into my bed. I'm staying in a six-bed dormitory and have been assigned the top bunk. The blanket and pillow were already there; I collected a pillowcase, duvet cover, and bedsheet from reception.
I also had to fill out an online registration form with details such as my nationality, identification number, and how I travelled to Stuttgart. Standard stuff.

There's an Indian guy in my room from Odisha. He's studying at THI and works as a working student here, so he comes to Stuttgart for one or two nights every week.

It's a simple place, but I'm settled in now. Tomorrow is the actual reason I'm here: my assessment at NEURA Robotics.

Let's see how that goes.


## October 16 — Assessment day

**6:30 a.m. — Good morning, Stuttgart**

I woke up at 6:30 a.m. and left around 7:40. The Indian guy from my room had already left at about 7:30.

I think two of the six beds were occupied by women, too. Not particularly important information, but apparently, this is the kind of thing I notice when I'm travelling.

One thing I forgot to mention last night: Stuttgart's U-Bahn system is quite different from Munich's. There are no digital route maps like the ones I'm used to, and the maps are positioned above the seats rather than above the doors. If people are sitting in front of them, it's inconvenient to check the route.

And the doors! You have to press a button to open them at the station, much like on a bus. Very strange.

I might have missed my stop last night if another passenger hadn't been getting off at the same station.


**8:24 a.m. — Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof, again**

I'm on the MEX18 towards Metzingen.

A few observations:

- No Wi-Fi at Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof or on the U-Bahn.
- The regional train is single-level.
- There are no tables.
- The route display doesn't give estimated arrival times.

I think I like Bavaria.


**9:15 a.m. — Metzingen bus station**

I'm waiting for bus 203. It's colder here than in Stuttgart. I just checked the temperature: seven degrees, compared with fourteen degrees in Stuttgart.

I really hope I don't freeze before the assessment.


**9:45 a.m. — Arrived at NEURA Robotics**

I've arrived! My assessment is scheduled for 10:15 a.m.

One person is already waiting for the same assessment. He's from India and is doing his master's in robotics in Hamburg.

The person I've exchanged emails with and spoken to on the phone comes out around 10 a.m. to greet me and asks me to wait. At 10:15, he returns to explain that there was an earlier issue and that there will be another 25–30-minute delay.

So, more waiting.

I've started reading *The Vanishing Cherry Blossom Bookshop*. At least I have something to do.


**11:00 a.m. — Still waiting**

Two more guys have arrived. One is from Egypt and studying mechatronics in Dortmund; the other is from somewhere in northern Germany and is doing a master's in embedded programming. Both are looking for voluntary internships.

It seems like quite a few people have travelled here for opportunities at NEURA.

My turn is finally approaching. I'm getting nervous, but also curious about what the assessment will involve.


**11:20 a.m. — Assessment complete**

Well, that was interesting!

I was taken into a room and fitted with a motion-capture suit. They attached sensors to it, and I had to control a robot with two arms by moving my own arms.

The task was simple in theory: there were packets and boxes on a table, and I had to put the packets into the left bag and the boxes into the right one. I had two minutes to practise and two minutes to perform the task.
The robot wasbulky and boxy. I had to keep my arms raised, which was a little strenuous on my shoulders. I also had to move my hands more slowly than I naturally wanted to.
I managed to sort everything except two packets that were positioned too close to the robot for me to bend its arm towards them properly. I don't know how well I did. I don't think they were particularly impressed with me or my performance, but perhaps I'm overthinking it.

The difficult part was that I had so little time to understand how the robot moved. They kept telling me to focus on picking up the items, so I couldn't really explore the range of motion or get a proper feel for the motors. The role itself isn't particularly technical, but I would genuinely love to be part of NEURA Robotics. Working around robots and seeing how this technology develops sounds exciting.

One thing I don't understand, though, is why they seem to be assessing so many students for what I assume is one position. People are travelling from far away, arranging accommodation, and spending two days on a trip for an assessment that lasts around twenty minutes.

Anyway, it's done now. There's nothing I can change about it.


**12:30 p.m. — On the bus back to Metzingen**

The bus is running five minutes late, so I'm not sure whether I'll catch my train.

I initially appreciated the digital display on the bus. It shows the weather, and the interface looks cleaner than the ones in Coburg. But I can't figure out why the bus-stop names keep moving around on the screen. I keep missing the next stop.

Also, the front section on the right side has one long seat instead of two separate ones. Oddly enough, I like that.

Apparently, I'm reviewing public transport now.


**12:55 p.m. — I may have just missed my train**

The bus was delayed, and I thought I'd miss my connection.

The stop names were once again appearing and disappearing on the screen. WTF. Hopefully I recognised what the bus stop at the bahnof looked like and thankfully, I did get off at the right place. But when I reached the platform, I saw a train leaving just as I arrived. For a moment, I thought I'd have to wait an entire hour.
Then I realised that was platform 3. My train was supposed to arrive at platform 2, and it showed up shortly afterwards.
Why are the platform numbers not displayed more clearly? Why must public transport test my patience like this?
Anyway, I'm on the train now.


**3:07 p.m. — Somewhere on the train to Würzburg**

I've just finished watching *As a Reincarnated Aristocrat, I'll Use My Appraisal Skill to Rise in the World*. It was fun, but now I don't know what to do with myself. I'll reach Coburg at 6:20 p.m., and I'm already getting bored. I suppose this is what happens when the exciting part of the trip is over and you're left with several hours of trains.


**4:39 p.m. — Würzburg Hauptbahnhof**

I'm on the RE20 towards Bamberg. It should have departed five minutes ago, but we're still waiting.
At this point, I think the trains are conspiring against me.


**5:49 p.m. — Bamberg Hauptbahnhof**

Waiting for the train to Coburg.
Home is getting closer.

**6:40 p.m. — Finally home**

I'm back!

I decided to walk to Aldi immediately because I know I'll probably have no energy to go shopping tomorrow. The sky was really pretty on my way home, with hints of pink and purple. I thought I'd take a picture from my room, then decided to take one downstairs instead.

Of course, I remembered the photograph only after it had become dark.

I went to Aldi and came back around 8:15 p.m. By then, I was truly *fix und fertig*.

Or at least, the travelling part.


## October 18 — The aftermath

**2:38 a.m. — Writing this from bed**

I slept at around 10 p.m. on the 16th and woke up yesterday, the 17th, at 9:30 a.m. I was too tired to do much for most of the day. By 5 p.m., I had a minor migraine. By 8:30 p.m., it had turned into a major one.

So much for coming home and being productive. I'm finally completing this blog now, but there's something I haven't quite been able to shake off.
I thought I'd love this trip. I was excited about travelling around Germany for the first time, staying in a hostel, visiting a new city, and possibly getting an internship at NEURA Robotics.

Instead, that twenty-minute assessment left me feeling strangely sad and exhausted.

I don't know why it affected me so much. Perhaps I'd built up the opportunity in my head. Perhaps I was simply tired. Or perhaps I wanted it to work out more than I realised. Whatever the reason, I came home feeling like a failure.

I know that sounds dramatic. I know a short assessment cannot determine my worth or measure everything I'm capable of doing. But knowing that doesn't automatically make the feeling disappear. I keep thinking about the journey, the waiting, the effort, and how much I wanted to do well. I don't even know whether my performance was bad. I just have this uncomfortable feeling that it wasn't enough.

Maybe I'll feel better after some rest.

For now, I think I'm going to stop trying to make sense of it and let myself recover.


## October 19 — The email

**9:39 a.m. — Update**

I've received an email from NEURA Robotics.

It says:

*“After careful consideration, we've decided to move forward with other candidates for the Data Operator Intern (Teleops) role, as their profiles are currently a slightly closer match for the position.”*

And there it is. The answer.

They've chosen someone else.

I can't say I'm surprised. Part of me had already expected this, though another part had been quietly hoping for a different outcome. I don't regret going to Stuttgart. I got to travel, experience a new place, stay in a hostel, and control a robot. Those things happened, and they were real. The trip wasn't meaningless just because I didn't get the internship.

But I'm still sad.

I think what hurts is not just the rejection itself. It's how hopeful I was before the trip, and how different I felt when I came home. I wanted this to be a little success story. Something I could look back on and say, *I took a chance, travelled all that way, and it worked out.*

Instead, I'm left with a tired body, a migraine, a half-finished blog, and an email that politely tells me I'm not the person they're looking for.
Maybe I'll try again somewhere else. Maybe this will matter less in a few days.
Right now, I don't feel particularly brave or inspired. I just feel disappointed, and a little lost.
I went to Stuttgart hoping to get one step closer to something I wanted. I came back home, I wasn't sure how to feel about myself.
I suppose that's where this story ends — not with a new beginning or a lesson learned, but with me trying to accept that sometimes you can make the effort, show up, and still not get the outcome you hoped for.

And that just hurts.
