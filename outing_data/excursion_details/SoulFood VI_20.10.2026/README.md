Today was another Soulfood day, and this time we were making vegetable Bolognese with pasta. For some reason, I keep pronouncing Bolognese as *Bolo Ganesh*, and honestly, I don't know if I'll ever be able to stop. 😂

When I arrived at Cosmos, the chef was nowhere to be found, and there was nobody at the counter either. So I went straight into the kitchen, where I found Max and Anna. What a nice surprise! We waited for about 15 minutes to see if anyone else would show up before getting started.

Meanwhile, we had to figure out what ingredients we needed. Piama was there too, and she asked us to buy Milchreis  and apple sauce so she could make ein Nachtisch for everyone.


## A Shopping Trip with Max and Anna

Max, Anna, and I headed to Norma to pick up the ingredients. We bought carrots, celery, pasta, Milchreis, apple sauce, and walnuts. Unfortunately, they didn't have rod lentil, so we had to got to  Tegut to get those.

This was actually my first time buying celery. I always see people in movies eating it, usually dipping it into yoghurt or something, but I'd somehow never realised what the whole thing looked like. Apparently, it's this big round-ish brown ball with green leaves on top. I had mostly seen the green part in my head, so seeing only the bottom part was a litle confusing.
We also found a pretty good deal on pasta: six packets for just €3! Naturally, we bought  six. According to Max, walnuts were the secret ingredient in the Bolognese, so we made sure to get those too.

Honestly, shopping with Max and Anna was a lot of fun. Anna has an incredible amount of energy, and somehow, she makes every conversation more entertaining. Max, meanwhile, is just cool in general. The whole trip felt less like running errands and more like hanging out with friends.

By the time we returned to Cosmos, Mustafa had arrived too. He'd come straight from work and  ready to get involved in the cooking. Time to get our hands clean before getting me dirty!


## Cooking Bolo Ganesh 

We washed our hands and divided the work between us. Mustafa chopped the onions, Anna took care of the garlic and celery, I chopped the carrots, and Piama got started on the dessert.

Piama's dessert was essentially rice, milk, and sugar — a German version of kheer. Once it was ready, she ate some with cherries and apple sauce before leaving early. Elly also left at some point while I was busy chopping carrots.

With the preparations out of the way, we finally started cooking. The Bolognese itself was pretty straightforward. We added the ingredients to a pot with some oil, let everything cook, and then added the red lentils and spices. Meanwhile, we boiled the penne pasta separately. It was nice seeing how everything came together. I'd never made Bolognese before, and it was interesting to learn a new way of cooking with lentils and vegetables.

But one of the best parts of cooking at Cosmos isn't just the food. It's the people you get to spend time with while making it.


### Mustafa and the Language Barrier

I've mentioned Mustafa before, but I have to say that I really enjoy his company. There's just one tiny problem: he doesn't speak English, and I can barely understand German. So, technically, we don't have a common language we can comfortably communicate in.

And yet, somehow, we manage.

He tries to use simple words and phrases to help me understand what he's saying, and for the most part, we just smile and nod at each other. Sometimes, that seems to be enough!
It's actually quite fun. We manage to communicate little things through gestures, expressions, and whatever words we happen to know. It's a reminder that you don't always need to speak the same language to enjoy someone's company.

Also, I completely forgot to mention that he's *really good-looking*. He gives off these fuzzy teddy-bear vibes, with great hair, a lovely beard, and a calm smile. There's something very warm and approachable about him.

Anyway, back to the food before this turns into a Mustafa appreciation post. 

By the time we finished cooking, Tetzian had also arrived, so we were ready to set up the table and eat.


## Dinner Time!

We laid everything out and sat down to eat. There was also a random kid hanging around who joined us for dinner.

There was another group of people whom Max had invited twice, but they hadn't joined us initially. Eventually, one girl from that group came over, ate some boiled pasta with salt, and left. Anna even asked if she wanted some ketchup, but she declined. I have to admit, watching someone casually eat raw pasta with salt was something. Everyone has their own way of enjoying food, I suppose!

Thankfully, she spread the word to her friends, and two or three more people eventually joined us. They actually ate the  pasta with Bolognese, which felt like a more conventional approach to the meal. As for the food itself, it was nice! But I think I'd probably enjoy the lentil-and-vegetable mixture more with rice. The way I see it, the Bolognese was essentially red lentils cooked with vegetables, and I feel like rice would complement it really well.

Something else I've noticed about these German food evenings is that I rarely feel completely full after eating. I usually arrive after having lunch, yet I still find myself going back for a second helping. Maybe it's just the portions, or maybe I'm simply someone who enjoys eating a lot. Either way, I'm not complaining!

After finishing my main dish, I decided to try the Milchreis.


## Trying Milchreis with All the Toppings

There were several toppings available, which made the whole thing more interesting.

The usual German accompaniments included Kirschen (cherries) and Apfelkompott (apple sauce). There were also coconut flakes, honey, and cinnamon. Mustafa mentioned that coconut and honey were toppings he ate back home, which I thought was interesting.

Naturally, I tried all of them. If I'm going to try something new, I might as well give it a fair chance!

Unfortunately, I have to say that Milchreis wasn't really for me. I appreciated trying it, and the different toppings were fun to experiment with, but I don't think I'll be rushing to make it myself anytime soon.

I'll happily stick to kheer for now!

Still, it was nice getting to try something that is a fairly common dessert here in Germany. It's always interesting to see how different cultures take similar ingredients — in this case, rice, milk, and sugar — and turn them into their own dishes.
