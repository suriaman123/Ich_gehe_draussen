Every October, the City of Coburg organises a Warentauschtag at the Alte Pakethalle, where people can bring things they no longer need and take home things they like. A big swap day, basically. One person's unwanted stuff is another person's treasure.

I was actually supposed to join Susanne on the Würzburg trip today, but I was feeling quite sad yesterday and ended up sleeping in this morning. Rather than sitting at home moping, I decided to go check out the Warentauschtag instead.

A little outing never hurt anyone, right?


**12:15 p.m. — Guterbahnhof bus stop**

It's drizzling, and the temperature is around minus two degrees, so I put on my heavy winter jacket. I took the bus from a nearby stop and noticed something: the front compartment on the right side has a single long seat, just like the buses I saw in Stuttgart. Now I'm wondering, has it always been like this and I've only just noticed, or are they rolling out a new bus design?

Anyway, I got off at the Guterbahnhof stop, why is it called Guterbahnhof when I can't see a Bahnhof nearby? A mystery for another day.The event was sopposed to start at 11 am, so I spotted a few people at the bus stop carrying all sorts of things, so I figured I was in the right place. The Alte Pakethalle was about a ten-minute walk away, and along the way, I saw plenty of people already heading home with their newly acquired treasures. There were also some teenagers, probably around sixteen, volunteering to help part the cars.

I was getting curious.


**12:25 p.m. — Inside the Alte Pakethalle**

Well, I don't know exactly what I was expecting, but it was a little underwhelming at first.

The electronics section wasn't particularly exciting. There were lots of decorative items and candle holders, plenty of crockery and kitchen utensils, and a whole lot of other things. I didn't need anything specific; I mainly wanted to see what was happening.
There were loads of books, though, so I picked up two children's books to begin with. There were also quilts, bags, skiing gear, and more.

Then I came across the clothing boxes.

There were so many jeans! And I actually need a new pair because I currently own just one pair of black jeans, and even those are starting to fade. But the thought of wearing someone else's jeans felt a little weird to me, so I decided I'd just buy a pair from TK Maxx or somewhere similar. I went back to the books instead.

I found a hardcover book called *Das große Buch*. It was in really good condition,  it was meant for children aged six and above,  and, I probably wouldn't understand even one per cent of it. So I left it behind, hoping some child would find it and enjoy it. However I found a few book to take home, which i hope i'll be able to read a little.

I did want to take a few pictures, but there were so many people around that I felt awkward pulling out my phone and photographing everything. My social anxiety is apparently still very much alive and well.

Another thing I realised while wandering around: I have a very specific winter-jacket problem. During summer, I completely forgot that I don't own a jacket suitable for temperatures between roughly -10°C and 5°C. The lighter jacket I own isn't warm enough, while the heavy one I'm wearing today is designed for temperatures closer to -10°C to -20°C. Last winter, temperatures dropped to around -19°C, so that the lowest temperature i have checked this jacket..
Now, if I wear a lighter jacket, I freeze. If I wear this one, I sweat like crazy.

There is apparently no middle ground in my wardrobe.

Anyway, back to the Warentauschtag.

Before leaving, I was checking out the children's toys when I noticed another section of the hall. This one was dedicated entirely to clothing, and most of the people browsing there seemed to be women. There were lots of clothes and some shoes, too. So, apparently, there was more to explore than I'd realised.

I left around 12:40 p.m. On my way back to the bus stop, I saw people carrying bags full of clothes. One guy had found a nice painting and a denim jacket, while someone else was carrying a lovely shawl.

And I went home with... well, nothing. ( i got some books, but after opening *Aufsitzehn... Vollstandige Reitlehre* i knew i cannot even understand children books)


**12:45 p.m. — Aldi, because I'm already outside**

Since I was out anyway, I decided to stop at Aldi to pick up groceries and a few other things.

There was a long queue at the checkout, so I opted for the self-checkout. I scanned everything, paid, and then the screen displayed:

*“Please wait. Help will be there shortly.”*

EXCUSE ME?

My money had been deducted. I'd scanned everything. What had I done wrong? Was something missing? Had I accidentally committed self-checkout fraud?

I stood there panicking internally until someone came over and refilled the receipt paper.

THAT WAS IT. THE RECEIPT PAPER.

Please, why would you give me a mini heart attack over printer paper?

I thanked the person with a perfectly normal “Danke schön” while internally recovering from the ordeal.

After Aldi, I went to dm because I wanted to buy yellow dal. I don't particularly like yellow dal, but Prince made a really delicious dal a couple of weeks ago and sent me some Instagram videos showing how to prepare it. So naturally, I wanted to try making it myself. I spent more than ten minutes searching for it, only to discover that red lentils cost €1.45 while yellow lentils cost €2.45.

And honestly? I don't even like yellow dal. So I decided not to buy it today. I'll try making red dal using the recipe from the Instagram video instead. If I end up craving yellow dal after that, I can buy it next week.

Look at me, making sensible financial decisions.


**On the bus home — Two unexpected encounters**

I took the bus towards Mohrenbrücke, and two rather different things happened.

First, there was a guy who got on the bus around the same time as me. He was tall, a little darker-skinned than me, looked vaguely South Asian (though I'm trying not to make assumptions based on appearances), and, well, looked good. When I got off at the Hochschule stop, he got off too. He said hi, and I said hi back.
Then he told me, “I've seen you somewhere, but I can't remember where we met.”
For a second, I thought he'd panicked after we made eye contact and was now trying to cover it up with a conversation. But then I remembered where I'd seen him: at KFC, when I went there for a trial assessment .

We chatted on the way home. I told him I'd been rejected for a cleaning job because I couldn't speak German. He said he didn't enjoy working there either and had quit. So now we're both looking for part-time jobs.

A very casual little conversation, but it was nice to bump into someone familiar.


The second encounter was a little different.

At the Bahnhof bus stop, three or four guys, probably South Asian, got on with an absolutely ridiculous amount of stuff. One guy had a completely stuffed hiking backpack. The side pockets, which would normally hold water bottles, contained formal shoes. He was also carrying a small mattress, another item I can't remember, and a unicorn plushie in hand.

A mattress AND a unicorn plushie. I respect the commitment.

Then I noticed Manish. He completely ignored me. Maybe he didn't recognise me, since we haven't seen each other since our German A1 exam in February. He had a vacuum cleaner, and I could see a couple of books sticking out of his bag.

Everyone seemed to have bags packed to the absolute limit.

And then I noticed the other passengers: a woman with bags full of clothes, a couple examining a jacket, people carrying all sorts of things.

It finally clicked.

**Everyone was coming back from the Warentauschtag.**

I'd completely forgotten that was today and i went there a couple of hours ago. And seeing all those things made me realise how much I'd probably missed by leaving after barely twenty minutes of browsing.

Maybe I should have stayed longer. Maybe there was another section I'd missed. Maybe I could have found something I actually liked.

Or maybe not.

Even if I'd stayed, I'm not sure I would have taken much. I feel a little strange about taking used clothes, and I'm quite particular about books. I usually prefer buying my own books and keeping them forever.

But books are expensive here, and I don't exactly have a permanent home where I can keep building my own little library.

So, you know... beggars can't be choosers.


**2:15 p.m. — Home sweet home**

I got home around 2:15 p.m. All in all, it took me about two hours to check out the Coburg Warentauschtag and do my shopping.

Not bad for an outing I almost didn't make. I went because I didn't want to sit at home feeling sad, and at least I got out of the house, saw something new, did some shopping, and had a couple of unexpected conversations.

I suppose that counts as a reasonably productive day.

**P.S.** I ended up getting a migraine afterwards and slept through the rest of the day.
So much for being productive. 😂
