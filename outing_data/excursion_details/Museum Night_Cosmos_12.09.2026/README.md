# Museum Night at Cosmos

**12.09.2026**

I reached Cosmos around **5:30 PM** and met Franzi there. We were working at the bar for the evening. Franzi took the orders, while I was in charge of making and serving the drinks.

I learned how to use a coffee machine. By the end of the evening, I had learned how to make **espresso, cappuccino, americano, hot chocolate, iced hot chocolate**, and even a **Wiener Schorle**. Normally, the Schorle is made with water, but if a customer wants it sweeter, we use Limo instead. So, not a bad amount of new drinks to learn in one evening!
I did make a few mistakes along the way, though. Twice, I didn't fit the coffee-ground holder into the machine properly, and both times it ended up making a bit of a mess. At one point, I also opened three Radlers instead of Pils. Thankfully, Franzi was really chill about all of it. She explained to the next customers that the Radlers were already open, and luckily, nobody had a problem with buying them. So, in the end, all went well. 😅
There was also one moment when Franzi went upstairs to take a video for Cosmos' Instagram account, leaving me alone at the bar. Usually, when I'm alone and a customer comes in, I say something like, “Meine Kollegin kommt in ein Minute. Bitte warte.” This time, though, I decided to try handling it myself. I pointed at all the drinks and managed to figure out what the customer wanted. The drink cost €3, but he gave me €4, so I guess I can officially say I made at least one sale on my own that evening! 😄

Ali also joined us for about two or three hours. At one point, Ali and Franzi started playing **Connect the Dots**, and I was helping Ali out. Despite my assistance, Franzi still managed to win. Apparently, Franzi is a pro.There was also a really nice man whose name I unfortunately don't remember. He gave me some leftover curry to take home, which was a really nice surprise.
One elderly customer had actually been to Pahadganj.After a brief conversation he decided to call me “India” for the rest of the evening. When he returned his empty bottle, he smiled and said, “Ciao, India!” and I replied, “Ciao, Deutschland!”. It was  a small interaction, but it was surprisingly funny and made me smile. 😄

And, of course, there were some perks to working there. I got to drink **iced tea and Limo for free**, and I also got dinner — **Chole Chawal on a Saturday night**. Definitely not complaining about that.

The reason I was volunteering at Cosmos was because it was **Museum Night**. There were different things on display, along with **two local bands performing** during the evening. The whole place had a really nice atmosphere, with people coming in to look around, listen to music, have drinks, and spend the evening together.We ended up making around **€226 in sales**.

Something I found particularly interesting was the way the payment system works at Cosmos. There are **three different prices** for things:
- **Standard** — the normal price of the drink or food.
- **Minimum** — a cheaper option, usually around €1, for people who don't have much money.
- **Support** — a slightly higher price, usually about €0.50 more than the standard price, for people who want to support the community.

Franzi had initially asked me if I wanted to take orders and explain this three-price system to customers. I'm actually pretty happy that I chose to make the drinks instead. I think I'd rather learn how to make coffee than explain three different prices to every customer all evening in Deutsch!

By around **11:30 PM**, I was getting pretty tired. I even dozed off for a little while when Franzi was counting the money. I eventually left around midnight.

It was a really fun evening, and I learned quite a few new things.

The only problem?

**My feet are still hurting.** 😅
