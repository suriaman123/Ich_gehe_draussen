
### Location: 50.26497480015601, 10.950651906929115
<!-- 26.10.2026 -->


I wasn't planning to write about this since it wasn't exactly an outing, but I figured I should record that I did something today. Maybe I'll add an archive feature to this page for small events like this.

I received an email saying someone from the **Bamberg Bundesagentur für Arbeit** would be available today to review application documents. It didn't specify which documents, just that students from our faculty were eligible.

From August to September, I applied for around 100 internships and received nothing but rejections. For the past two weeks, I've been meaning to improve my resume but kept procrastinating. I even asked ChatGPT and Claude to review it. They rated it 4.5 and 5, respectively, but the resumes they generated were somehow even worse than mine.

I made my current resume in China a few months ago. The main issue is my Excudate project, which doesn't really fit my course or the jobs I'm applying for. Unfortunately, I don't have another project to replace it with. Both AI models suggested turning my work experience into bullet points, but that would mean removing the project entirely. My resume is already packed.

So I thought, why not ask a professional?

I scheduled an email for 8 a.m., introducing myself and explaining that I was looking for an internship. I asked for an appointment and mentioned that I'd drop by around noon if I didn't hear back.

No reply came, so I went anyway.

It took me a minute to find room 1-005. I arrived at 12:18, but another student was already there, so I was asked to return in ten minutes. I wandered around the nearby hallway and came back at 12:35.

She was friendly and welcoming. She'd initially been speaking German quite quickly, and when I said *Ich spreche nicht Deutsch*, she panicked for a second. The classic German response followed: “Sorry, my English is not very good.” This time, there was probably some truth to it. She later mentioned that she'd learnt English at school 30 years ago and hadn't needed to use it much since.

Thankfully, I'd brought my German resume. She read it carefully and said it was good and didn't need any  changes. She also explained that finding jobs where English alone is enough can be difficult, and the job market isn't as good as it was three years ago. Her advice was simple: **work on my German.**

She was clearly uncomfortable speaking English and occasionally used a translator on her laptop to find the right words, but she genuinely tried. I really appreciated that.

Fun little detail: she was using a Lenovo ThinkPad with a built-in card reader on the side, and her ID card was inserted into it. For some reason, I found that interesting.

I thanked her and left around 12:50.

I don't think the meeting changed much, but at least I can stop stressing about my resume for a while. And it was my first time on campus during classes this semester, so seeing so many students around felt a little strange.

Not a particularly exciting day, but at least I got something done.
