I arrived at Cosmos at my usual time, around 16:10, and met Tetzi and Max. Max, speaking in a French accent, announced that we would be making *lasang* — lasagna!

He also told me there was a concert tomorrow, so we'd be making extra for the catering. He invited me to volunteer, which meant free entry and, of course, a free drink. Not a bad deal!

Mustafa arrived around 16:30. Franzi was at the counter, and Anna was there too. We made a shopping list and headed to Norma. Since Max also needed supplies for the café and tomorrow's event, he brought out the little shopping cart.

I felt like an American kid bringing his toys to the playground.

On the way to Norma, Max sat in the cart while Mustafa pulled him along. Honestly, I always forget how much fun Max and Anna are. We bought everything we could find and split into two groups. Anna, Mustafa, and I returned to Cosmos with the cart, while Tetzi and Max went to Tegu for lasagna sheets, vegan cheese, and some Melasse-Hefeflocken.

When we got back, Piama was already there, and it was time to cook!


## The great lasagna operation

Lasagna has two main components: the Bolognese sauce and the white sauce.

Piama took charge of the white sauce. She started with oil, garlic, Lorbeerblätter(bay leaves — even in English, I don't know what that is), paprika powder, and a truly impressive amount of flour. Then came three litres of oat milk, vegetable stock, pommes-frites seasoning, and garlic powder.

And that was our vegan white sauce sorted. 

Meanwhile, we got to work on the vegetables. I chopped carrots, Mustafa handled the onions, and Anna cut celery — the green parts this time. I even saved the carrot tops for Tetzi, whose neighbour has a pet rabbit. Nothing goes to waste! On the other side, Tetzi was peeling and grating beetroot for the salad. There were three or four of them, so once he'd had enough, Max took over. I chopped walnuts, which as we know is  the secret ingredient. At this point, I finally gathered the courage to ask Max where the chef was. I now officially know her name is **Judith**, and she's on vacation. I'll probably still call her Chef here, though.

Anna had started preparing the Bolognese sauce. Oil, tomato paste, vegetables, red lentils, passata passierte Tomaten, Italian seasoning, and vegetable stock — all the usual suspects. The twist this time was about 400 ml of grape juice and some vinegar.

My job became stirring the pot. Very important work, obviously.

Meanwhile, Tetzi, Max, and Mustafa assembled the salad with beetroot, oranges, apples, walnuts, pomegranate, oil, grape juice, and several other things. It looked like the entire fruit and vegetable section had decided to attend the same party.


## Ice cream before dinner?

Then Anna walked in eating ice cream.

I looked at her sadly.

“Are you sad because you don't have ice cream?” she asked.

I nodded.

And she actually went and got me one! She brought me to the back room, where a lovely woman — I think she works at Cosmos and was the one who brought the dragon when I arrived today — let me choose whatever I wanted.

I chose a cone.

There I was, a grown adult who'd spent the afternoon hauling shopping cart and chopping vegetables, now eating ice cream before dinner like a five-year-old. Honestly, a perfect evening.


## Finally, lasagna!

Once the Bolognese was ready, we started assembling the trays: Bolognese, lasagna sheets, white sauce, and repeat. Tetzi mixed cheese with milk for the topping, which went over the final layer.

At around 19:00, we put everything in the oven for 30 minutes. Usually, I'm back home by six after Soulfood. Today, dinner was running late, and by the time I finally sat down, I was exhausted.

Franzi had left around 18:00, and Max invited everyone else who was around to join us, but nobody came. So it was just us.

At 19:30, the lasagna was ready. We sat down and ate together. It was piping hot and absolutely delicious.

Everyone was using a fork and knife, but I liked Anna's approach best: she ate it like pasta, using just a fork and mixing everything together. Much more practical, if you ask me.

I wanted another serving, but I eat a little slowly, and I didn't want to be the only person still eating at the table. While putting my bowl away, I tried a little of the salad. Let's just say I'm glad I only took half a spoon.

By then, it was 19:58, and my bus was at 20:06. I really didn't feel like walking home, so I skipped helping with the dishwasher and left with Mustafa and Piama.

I reached home at 20:36.


## Look how far I've come!

It was a fun evening, but a tiring one.

What's funny is that just last month, I was making pasta with pesto alongside Chef — basically cooking level one. And now I'm helping prepare lasagna for a group of people, working alongside five others, and learning how an actual kitchen operates.

I guess I've come a long way.

Between riding in shopping carts, getting free ice cream, and making enough lasagna to feed a small army, today was a good day.
