# The Job Interview I Didn't Know I Had

**16.09.2026**

Yesterday, I got a phone call from a lady speaking German. I understood absolutely nothing. After a few seconds of confusion, I finally said, *“Ich spreche nicht Deutsch.”* She immediately switched to English and said, **“You applied for a job. Is tomorrow at 12 PM okay?”** I was like, *sure, I guess?* I asked her to send me the precise address, because I had applied to quite a few jobs randomly and honestly had no idea who she was or which job she was talking about.

A little later, she emailed me the address. I saw **SPS Direkt**, googled it, and realised it was a temporary employment agency that provides workers to different companies. At this point, I assumed I must have applied for working student job as a office employee. And naturally, my brain immediately started planning my future. I started thinking about **work, money, what I was going to wear to the interview**, and all the other things you think about when you suddenly believe you have a job opportunity.

Then I checked my Excel sheet where I keep track of my job applications. It was for a **winter employee** position. Initially, I thought that probably meant they needed some extra workers during the Christmas season or something like that. But while doing some more research, I found the job advertisement on XING and finally read the actual job description. Turns out, “winter employee” meant **removing snow from roads and putting down sand to increase traction for cars**. And this would apparently happen either **very early in the morning or very late at night**. Suddenly, I was not nearly as excited about the job as I had been five minutes earlier. Still, I thought I should at least go to the interview.


## The Morning of the Interview

I woke up at 8 am, with my interview at noon and needing to leave around 10. I freshened up, took a shower, heated up some leftover rice and chole, and got ready. The only problem was that I had a headache, and I started wondering if I should even go.Eventually, I decided to just take the chance and leave.

I was half asleep throughout the train journey, but honestly, I think that helped. By the time I reached Sonneberg at around 11 am, my headache had gone down to maybe 10%. For someone who suffers from sever migrain pain, that's basically close to nothing. I had about an hour before the interview, so I stayed at the railway station for around 30 minutes before heading over.

When I arrived, the receptionist asked me to sit in a room and gave me a form to fill out. I was almost finished when **Mrs. Nina** came in. We talked for a while, and she filled in the remaining parts of the form herself. I told her that I would ideally like to find something related to my studies. She said she would like to give me something in that area, but unfortunately, she doesn't get many requests for that kind of work. So, in the end, we settled on **factory work**. She currently has a client looking for workers to put packed cheese into boxes. The factory is in a pretty remote location, but there is a shuttle service for the workers. I told her that I could do that kind of work. She said she would contact me if I could join that position or if something similar became available. So, no job yet — but at least something might come out of it.


## The Journey Back

After the interview, I went back to the railway station and waited about an hour for my train. To pass the time, I started watching **Chumbak**, which my brother had recommended. Once I got on the train, I was asleep for pretty much the entire journey again. When I reached home, I was wearing formal shoes and had originally planned to take a bus. But the next bus wasn't coming for another **25 minutes**, so I decided to just walk instead.

All in all, it was a surprisingly productive outing for something that started with me not even knowing what job I had applied for.

Now I just have to wait and see if they call me.

Oh, and yes...

**I still have a headache.**
