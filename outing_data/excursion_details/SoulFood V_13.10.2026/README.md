The plan for today was pretty straightforward: go to Cosmos, help make some food, and follow one simple rule — if we added onions, I wouldn't eat it; if we didn't, I would. After that, I wanted to leave by 6 PM, head to Aldi to buy capsicum, lots of milk, and a few other things, and then make it to the screening of Don't Look Up for an hour. They were giving away popcorn, a drink, and a goodie bag, so that sounded like a pretty good deal.

Well, things didn't exactly go according to plan. But honestly, I think the day turned out pretty well anyway.

## A Rainy Start

I left at my usual time, but it was raining. I was already dressed and out of the house, so I decided to continue rather than turn back. I waited for the bus for 13 minutes, by which point my jacket was already wet. Not the most promising start to the evening! When I finally reached Cosmos, I found a lot of elderly people there. Apparently, they all come together once a month to cook, and today happened to be their day. There was also a lady who spoke English. She was probably in her late 20s or early 30s — though I initially wanted to say mid-20s before remembering that I'm in my mid-20s myself. Funny how that works!

Elly was at the counter, so I greeted her before we all sat down to discuss what we were going to cook.


## Making Udon Noodles with Tofu

The English-speaking lady suggested making udon noodles with tofu, which immediately got my attention. I've been craving tofu ever since coming to Germany, and if it was anything like the pan-fried tofu I used to eat in Shanghai, I was going to have a good day.

We got started with the preparations. We cut the bell pepper and cucumber into long strips, grated the carrots into long shreds, and chopped the tofu into small cubes. Then Tetzian and I shallow-fried the tofu before adding a mixture of maple syrup, soy sauce, and spices. Meanwhile, the extra-thick udon noodles were boiled separately. The lady made a dressing using maple syrup, soy sauce, and yoghurt, and we also roasted a sesame seed mix, which surprisingly added a lot of flavour. Tetzian did most of the actual work, while I mostly hung around and helped where needed. Let's just say I was more of a supporting cast member than the main chef!

Once everything was ready, we sat down to eat.

The dish was essentially a Chinese/Japanese-inspired meal made by Germans, and, well, it tasted like it. 😂 It was nice, but I still prefer the tofu I had in China. Maybe I'm just missing Shanghai a little. As soon as I spotted Elly, I invited her to join us. Piama and Franzi were there too. Piama and Elly loved the food, while Franzi didn't try it.

All in all, it was a nice meal, and I'd learned how to make a new dish. Not bad for an evening that had started with a wet jacket and a bus delay!


## An Unexpected Invitation

By around 6 PM, I put on my jacket, ready to leave. The plan was to head to Aldi and then catch the screening of Don't Look Up. But just as I was about to go, Elly and Franzi asked if I wanted to stay and play a game. Apparently, it was game night. I figured I could stay for one game. What could possibly go wrong?

We sat down — Elly, Franzi, Piama, and I — to play Labyrinth. It was the only game available that didn't require me to know German, which made it the obvious choice. A new guy named Mustafa joined us. He understood English but didn't speak it, so communication was a little limited, but we managed. The game was fun! Piama won the first round, and I won the second. A pretty good start to game night, if I say so myself.

## Cards, Competition, and Some New Words

After a while, Piama left, and Tetzian joined us. We switched to a card game called Asshole.

The rules were fairly simple: you had to play cards of the same value or higher than the previous player and try to get rid of all the cards in your hand. Simple enough in theory, although things got a little competitive. Elly won the first round but had to leave when the chef called her away. Franzi wasn't particularly happy about that! Franzi then won the second round before leaving too.

Eventually, it was just the three of us guys sitting around and talking. Mustafa is Afghan, and at some point, he started teaching us a few swear words in Afghan. We had quite a conversation after that, and it was nice getting to know him a little better. Before I knew it, it was 7:30 PM. Mustafa and Franzi had started another game with two other people, while I decided it was finally time to head home.

I said goodbye to everyone and left.


## Goodbye, Movie Night. Hello, Sleep.

I reached home at 8:14 PM. At that point, there didn't seem to be much point in going to the movie anymore, so I decided to call it a day and go to bed early instead. The original plan had been to cook, shop, and watch a movie. Instead, I cooked (well, helped cook), ate something new, played two new games, met someone new, and spent the evening chatting with people I might not otherwise have spoken to.

I didn't get my shopping done, and I missed the movie screening. But I did get to spend a nice evening with people, learn a new recipe, and get to know Mustafa a little better.

All in all, a happy day.

Sometimes, the best days are the ones that don't go according to plan.

Tomorrow, though, there's no escaping it — I definitely have to go shopping after the Creapolis workshop. My pantry is quite empty at this point.
