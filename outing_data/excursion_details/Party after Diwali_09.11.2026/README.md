I'm starting this story a little before the actual event. I'm not entirely sure why, but I suppose that's how this story goes.

I was still recovering from the GO! event and woke up at around 12 PM. I'm not sure when exactly, but Pinto was already gone, and I was absolutely starving. After getting something to eat, I messaged him and found out that he had left at around 6 AM. Apparently, my phone had rung, and I had turned it off. I have absolutely no memory of doing that.

I also messaged Manish. Here's the exact message I sent him:
> Hi Manish, this is Aman from AIMS.  
> Firstly belated Happy Diwali bro 🎆🎆🎆  
> Secondly, yesterday Adarsh was staying with me and got some sweets from you. I got to taste them and they tasted Indian. I just want to thank you that I got to taste a little India on Diwali. Thanks ☺️☺️

After that, I went back to sleep. Not sure for how long, but eventually I woke up to find out that Manish had actually made the sweets himself. He also offered me some more since he had extras.

So, at around 20:00, I went over to his room.

A few things about his room: it's huge! He has also converted one of the pantry shelves into a small mandir, which looked quite nice. He still had a small pooja setup on his table from the previous day, so the Diwali atmosphere was very much alive.

I took some sweets and gave him a Kinder Joy in exchange. Seems like a fairly decent deal to me.

We then sat down and started talking. And by talking, I mean we talked for more than an hour. Our conversation covered internships, college life, part-time jobs and, of course, women. Apparently, Manish is 29, and his family has started looking for potential matches for him. He recently received an offer from a girl's family. The girl is currently pursuing her BTech, so there seems to be a bit of an age difference. His reasoning, however, was that since she is young, there is a good chance she has had next to zero boyfriends, which is apparently important to him. Well, if both the girl and the boy consent, who am I to judge?

He is also going to the gym regularly and is a vegetarian who somehow manages to keep up with his protein intake. I still cannot understand how people manage to do all these things. I can barely manage to get up in the morning!

At around 21:10, his friends called. I was getting hungry again, so I tried to excuse myself. However, Manish stopped me. His friends had brought some firecrackers, and he wanted me to stay. I agreed, although I first went back to my room to put on some shoes and eat something.

I then headed downstairs again and met all of Manish's friends. They were all Indians and studying at FMS as well. They seemed to express their affection through constant banter. I didn't really get involved in it, though. I'll leave that department to them.
We walked towards the Aula, which is more or less the starting / ending point of the college. There, we decided to set off some firecrackers.

Well, technically, only one guy set them off while the rest of us watched.

Firecrackers in Germany are a bit disappointing, to be honest. They burn for approximately two seconds, and that's it. We lit a few, but they started leaving marks on the stone floor, so we decided to stop. We then started walking back home, although the guy who had the remaining crackers continued setting off a few along the way.

We also took a group photo outside the dorm. I'm trying to get more comfortable with having my pictures taken, so that's another small step in that direction.

On the way back, I stopped by Utkarsh's place because I wanted to talk to him. He gave me some leftover kheer from the Diwali party, which was a nice bonus. We talked for a bit. He is also starting his thesis and going to the gym regularly. Now I am starting to think all FM guys think alike. Unfortunately, he was about to leave to meet a friend, so I didn't stay for too long and headed back.

I finally reached my room at around 22:50, absolutely starving.

And that, more or less, was my party after Diwali. Nothing particularly grand, but a nice evening filled with Indian sweets, conversations, new acquaintances, a little bit of fireworks and, most importantly, a reminder of home.
